\section{The Carter regrouping identity, term by term}
\label{app:carterfull}

Chapter~\ref{ch:geodesics} asserts the identity \eqref{eq:regroup} that
makes the Hamilton--Jacobi equation separate; here is the verification
in full, since the whole architecture rests on it. Multiply both sides
of \eqref{eq:regroup} by $\Delta$ and expand the right-hand side:
\begin{align}
-P^2 + \Delta\left(aE\sin\theta - \frac{L_z}{\sin\theta}\right)^{\!2}
={}& -\bigl[E(r^2+a^2) - aL_z\bigr]^2 \nonumber\\
&{}+ \Delta\left(a^2E^2\sin^2\theta - 2aEL_z
+ \frac{L_z^2}{\sin^2\theta}\right) .
\end{align}
Collect by powers of $E$ and $L_z$:
\begin{align}
E^2:&\quad -(r^2+a^2)^2 + a^2\Delta\sin^2\theta = -A
&&\text{(definition of $A$)},\nonumber\\
EL_z:&\quad 2a(r^2+a^2) - 2a\Delta = 2a\bigl[r^2+a^2-\Delta\bigr]
= 4Mar
&&\text{(since $r^2+a^2-\Delta = 2Mr$)},\nonumber\\
L_z^2:&\quad -a^2 + \frac{\Delta}{\sin^2\theta}
&&\text{(as it stands)} .
\label{eq:regroupterms}
\end{align}
The three collected coefficients are exactly the numerator entries of
the raw contraction \eqref{eq:hjraw}, term for term, which proves
\eqref{eq:regroup}. Note what did the work: the definition of $A$
absorbed the $\theta$-dependence of the $E^2$ sector, and the identity
$r^2 + a^2 - \Delta = 2Mr$ — the statement that $g_{t\phi}$ is
proportional to $Mar$ — collapsed the cross term. Both are properties
of the specific Kerr combination of metric functions; a generic
stationary axisymmetric metric leaves an irreducible mixed remainder,
which is precisely why the quasi-Kerr spacetime of
Chapter~\ref{ch:nohair} has no Carter constant and must be integrated
by Christoffel symbols.

\section{The equatorial photon-orbit cubic}
\label{app:photoncubic}

For equatorial photons ($Q = 0$), write $b = L_z/E$ and factor $E^2$
out of the radial potential \eqref{eq:Req}:
\begin{equation}
R(r)/E^2 = (r^2 + a^2 - ab)^2 - \Delta\,(b-a)^2 .
\end{equation}
A circular orbit needs $R = 0$ and $R' = 0$:
\begin{align}
(r^2 + a^2 - ab)^2 &= \Delta\,(b-a)^2,
\label{eq:appR0}\\
4r\,(r^2 + a^2 - ab) &= 2(r - M)(b-a)^2.
\label{eq:appR1}
\end{align}
Taking the square root of \eqref{eq:appR0} (both sides positive for
$r > r_+$) gives $r^2 + a^2 - ab = \sqrt{\Delta}\,|b - a|$; substituting
into \eqref{eq:appR1} yields
$4r\sqrt{\Delta} = 2(r-M)|b-a|$, i.e.
\begin{equation}
|b - a| = \frac{2r\sqrt{\Delta}}{r - M},
\qquad
r^2 + a^2 - ab = \frac{2r\,\Delta}{r - M}.
\label{eq:appbelim}
\end{equation}
Eliminating $b$ is now mechanical. From the first relation,
$b = a \pm 2r\sqrt{\Delta}/(r-M)$; substituting into the second,
\begin{equation}
r^2 + a^2 - ab
= r^2 \mp \frac{2ar\sqrt{\Delta}}{r-M}
\stackrel{!}{=} \frac{2r\,\Delta}{r-M} ,
\end{equation}
and multiplying through by $(r-M)/r$:
\begin{equation}
r(r-M) \mp 2a\sqrt{\Delta} = 2\Delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mp 2a\sqrt{\Delta} = r^2 - 3Mr + 2a^2 .
\end{equation}
Squaring and inserting $\Delta = r^2 - 2Mr + a^2$, the $a^4$ terms
cancel and a common factor $r$ drops out:
\begin{equation}
r^3 - 6Mr^2 + 9M^2 r - 4a^2 M = 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\bigl(x^3 - 3x\bigr)^2 = 4a_*^2,
\qquad x = \sqrt{r/M},
\end{equation}
whose two square-root branches are the prograde/retrograde pair
\begin{equation}
x^3 - 3x \pm 2a_* = 0 .
\end{equation}
With three real roots guaranteed for $|a_*| \le 1$ (the discriminant
$4\cdot27(1 - a_*^2) \ge 0$), the trigonometric solution of a depressed
cubic $x^3 - 3px + q = 0$, $x = 2\sqrt p\cos\vartheta$ with
$\cos 3\vartheta = -q/2p^{3/2}$, gives directly
\begin{equation}
x = 2\cos\!\Bigl[\tfrac13\cos^{-1}(\mp a_*)\Bigr]
\;\Longrightarrow\;
r_{\rm ph}^{\pm} = 4M\cos^2\!\Bigl[\tfrac13\cos^{-1}(\mp a_*)\Bigr]
= 2M\Bigl\{1 + \cos\!\Bigl[\tfrac23\cos^{-1}(\mp a_*)\Bigr]\Bigr\},
\end{equation}
the form implemented in \texttt{r\_photon}. The same cubic
$x^3 - 3x + 2a_*$ reappears as the denominator polynomial of the
Page--Thorne flux (Section~\ref{app:pagethorne}) — not a coincidence:
both express where the circular-orbit denominator
$1 - 3M/r \pm 2a\sqrt{M/r^3}$ of
\eqref{eq:ecirc}--\eqref{eq:lcirc} degenerates.

\section{The BPT ISCO reduction}
\label{app:isco}

Stability of a circular timelike orbit requires, in addition to
$R(r) = R'(r) = 0$, that $R''(r) \le 0$ (the orbit sits at a potential
minimum); the ISCO is the marginal case $R'' = 0$. Substituting the
circular-orbit $E(r), L(r)$ of \eqref{eq:ecirc}--\eqref{eq:lcirc} into
$R''(r) = 0$ and simplifying — the step-by-step reduction is
straightforward but long; \citet{BardeenPressTeukolsky1972} give the
intermediate identities — collapses the condition to the quartic
\begin{equation}
r^2 - 6Mr \mp 8a\sqrt{Mr} - 3a^2 = 0
\label{eq:iscoquartic}
\end{equation}
(upper sign prograde), a quartic in $\sqrt r$. BPT observed that the
substitutions
\begin{equation}
Z_1 = 1 + (1-a_*^2)^{1/3}\bigl[(1+a_*)^{1/3} + (1-a_*)^{1/3}\bigr],
\qquad
Z_2 = \sqrt{3a_*^2 + Z_1^2}
\end{equation}
solve it in closed form:
$r_{\rm ISCO}/M = 3 + Z_2 \mp \sqrt{(3-Z_1)(3+Z_1+2Z_2)}$. Verification
is mechanical: substitute and use
$(1-a_*^2)^{1/3}$-algebra to confirm \eqref{eq:iscoquartic} vanishes
identically — the unit tests do the numerical equivalent at fifteen
digits across the spin range, including the exact anchor points
$r_{\rm ISCO}(0) = 6M$, $r_{\rm ISCO}^{+}(1) = M$,
$r_{\rm ISCO}^{-}(1) = 9M$.

\subsection{The ISCO energy identity}
\label{app:eisco}

Chapter~\ref{ch:kerr} promotes the identity
$E_{\rm ISCO} = \sqrt{1 - 2M/(3\,r_{\rm ISCO})}$
\eqref{eq:eisco}. In the Schwarzschild case it is a two-line
computation worth seeing. With $a = 0$,
\begin{equation}
E^2(r) = \frac{(1 - 2M/r)^2}{1 - 3M/r},
\end{equation}
and the marginal-stability quartic \eqref{eq:iscoquartic} reduces to
$r = 6M$, so
\begin{equation}
E^2_{\rm ISCO} = \frac{(1 - 1/3)^2}{1 - 1/2} = \frac{4/9}{1/2}
= \frac{8}{9} = 1 - \frac{2M}{3\cdot 6M} .
\end{equation}
For general spin the same statement follows from
\eqref{eq:ecirc} once the marginal condition \eqref{eq:iscoquartic} is
used to eliminate the $a\sqrt{M/r^3}$ combinations
\citep[the intermediate identities are eqs.~2.12--2.21
of][]{BardeenPressTeukolsky1972}; rather than transcribe that page of
algebra we record the numerical confirmation, which is independent of
any transcription: across the \texttt{physics\_tables} spin grid the
identity and the general formula agree to all printed digits (e.g.\
$0.820404$ vs $0.820406$ at $\astar = 0.938$, the residual being the
grid's own rounding), including both rotation senses.

\section{The Page--Thorne flux integral}
\label{app:pagethorne}

The energy-angular-momentum balance of
Section~\ref{sec:thindiskstructure} gives the flux as
\begin{equation}
F(r) = \frac{\dot M}{4\pi r}\,
\frac{-\Omega'(r)}{\bigl(E - \Omega L\bigr)^2}
\int_{r_{\rm ISCO}}^{r}\bigl(E - \Omega L\bigr)L'\,\mathrm{d}r',
\label{eq:ptintegral}
\end{equation}
with all quantities the circular-orbit functions of
Chapter~\ref{ch:kerr}. The substitution $x = \sqrt{r/M}$ (and units
$M = 1$) turns every circular-orbit function into a ratio of low-order
polynomials in $x$ over the \emph{same} square root:
\begin{equation}
\Omega = \frac{1}{x^3 + a_*},
\qquad
E = \frac{x^3 - 2x + a_*}{x^{3/2}\sqrt{x^3 - 3x + 2a_*}},
\qquad
L = \frac{x^4 - 2a_* x + a_*^2}{x^{3/2}\sqrt{x^3 - 3x + 2a_*}} .
\label{eq:ptcircx}
\end{equation}
The combination the integrand needs then telescopes beautifully:
\begin{align}
E - \Omega L
&= \frac{(x^3 - 2x + a_*)(x^3 + a_*) - (x^4 - 2a_*x + a_*^2)}
{(x^3 + a_*)\,x^{3/2}\sqrt{x^3 - 3x + 2a_*}} \nonumber\\
&= \frac{x^3\,(x^3 - 3x + 2a_*)}
{(x^3 + a_*)\,x^{3/2}\sqrt{x^3 - 3x + 2a_*}}
= \frac{x^{3/2}\,\sqrt{x^3 - 3x + 2a_*}}{x^3 + a_*} ,
\label{eq:eminusomegal}
\end{align}
the square root cancelling into the numerator (the middle equality is
a direct expansion; every term not proportional to
$x^3 - 3x + 2a_*$ cancels identically). Equation
\eqref{eq:eminusomegal} is the engine of the whole closed form: the
integrand $(E - \Omega L)L'$ becomes a rational function whose
denominator is $x^3 - 3x + 2a_*$ — factorable over the roots
\eqref{eq:ptroots} — so the integral evaluates by partial fractions to
one power term, one logarithm from the $a_*$-linear piece, and one
logarithm per root:
\begin{equation}
\int \;\longrightarrow\;
x - x_0 - \tfrac32 a_*\ln\frac{x}{x_0}
- \sum_{i=1}^{3}
\frac{3(x_i - a_*)^2}{x_i(x_i - x_j)(x_i - x_k)}
\ln\frac{x - x_i}{x_0 - x_i},
\end{equation}
which is the bracket of \eqref{eq:pagethorne}; the prefactor
$1/[x^2(x^3 - 3x + 2a_*)]$ collects
$-\Omega'/(E - \Omega L)^2$ and the $1/r$ of \eqref{eq:ptintegral}.
The Schwarzschild guard of Chapter~\ref{ch:disk} arises because
$x_2 \to 0$ as $a_* \to 0$ while its residue coefficient diverges as
$1/x_2$; the product tends to the finite limit that the code takes
analytically.

\subsection{The far-field constant, exactly}
\label{app:farfield}

The far-field expansion asserted by the unit test —
$x - c + 3/x + \mathcal{O}(x^{-2})$ with $c \simeq 3.976$ at
$a_* = 0$, \emph{not} the Newtonian constant — can be closed in
elementary terms. At $a_* = 0$ the cubic's roots are
$x_1 = \sqrt3$, $x_2 = 0$, $x_3 = -\sqrt3$, and
$x_0 = \sqrt{r_{\rm ISCO}/M} = \sqrt6$. The $x_2$ term vanishes (the
guard limit), and the two surviving residues evaluate to
\begin{equation}
\frac{3(\pm\sqrt3)^2}{(\pm\sqrt3)(\pm\sqrt3)(\pm2\sqrt3)}
= \pm\frac{\sqrt3}{2},
\end{equation}
so the bracket is
\begin{equation}
x - \sqrt6
- \frac{\sqrt3}{2}\ln\frac{x-\sqrt3}{\sqrt6-\sqrt3}
+ \frac{\sqrt3}{2}\ln\frac{x+\sqrt3}{\sqrt6+\sqrt3} .
\end{equation}
As $x \to \infty$,
$\ln\bigl[(x-\sqrt3)/(x+\sqrt3)\bigr] \to -2\sqrt3/x + \mathcal
O(x^{-3})$, which supplies exactly the $+3/x$ term of the expansion,
and the constants collect into
\begin{equation}
c = \sqrt6 + \frac{\sqrt3}{2}
\ln\frac{\sqrt6+\sqrt3}{\sqrt6-\sqrt3}
= 2.4495 + 0.8660\times1.7629 = 3.9762 ,
\label{eq:farfieldc}
\end{equation}
the ``$c \simeq 3.976$'' of \eqref{eq:farfield} now an exact closed
form. The Newtonian folklore profile corresponds to dropping the two
logarithms — i.e.\ to $c = \sqrt6 \approx 2.449$ — and the difference
between the two constants is precisely the general-relativistic
torque bookkeeping near the inner edge, which is why asserting the
correct $c$ in a unit test guards physics no rendered image can.

\section{The quasi-Kerr structure functions at large radius}
\label{app:htseries}

The \citet{GlampedakisBabak2006} functions $F_1(r), F_2(r)$ have the
form $p_n(M/r) + q_n(M/r)\,\ln(1 - 2M/r)$ with low-order polynomials
$p_n, q_n$, arranged so the quadrupole correction to the metric falls
as $(M/r)^3$. Expanding the logarithm,
$\ln(1-2M/r) = -2M/r - 2(M/r)^2 - \tfrac83(M/r)^3 - \dots$, the
polynomial and logarithmic contributions cancel through the first five
orders; in double precision the relative error of the direct evaluation
therefore grows as $\sim \varepsilon_{\rm mach}(r/M)^5$ — unusable
beyond $r \sim 50\,M$ and catastrophic at S2 radii. The implementation
switches at $r = 9M$ to the explicit series of the \emph{cancelled}
result (coefficients from the symbolic expansion, kept to
$\mathcal{O}((M/r)^8)$), with the branch-continuity unit test asserting
agreement of the two branches at the joint to $10^{-10}$.

For the record, the exact covariant deviation the code applies
(\texttt{HartleThorneMetric::components\_impl}) is, with
$f = 1 - 2M/r$ and the quadrupole angular factor
$1 - 3\cos^2\theta = -2P_2(\cos\theta)$:
\begin{align}
\delta g_{tt} &= +\epsq\, f\,(1 - 3\cos^2\theta)\,F_1(r),
\nonumber\\
\delta g_{rr} &= +\epsq\,(1 - 3\cos^2\theta)\,F_1(r)/f,
\nonumber\\
\delta g_{\theta\theta} &= -\epsq\, r^2 (1 - 3\cos^2\theta)\,F_2(r),
\nonumber\\
\delta g_{\phi\phi} &= -\epsq\, r^2\sin^2\theta\,
(1 - 3\cos^2\theta)\,F_2(r),
\label{eq:htdeviation}
\end{align}
added to the exact Kerr components (with the $\epsq = 0$ early-return
making the Kerr limit exact by construction rather than by
cancellation). The overall sign is anchored \emph{empirically}, not
notationally: \citet{GlampedakisBabak2006} define $h^{ab}$ as a
perturbation of the inverse metric, so a naive index-lowering flips
the sign relative to \eqref{eq:htdeviation}, and the convention that
counts is the physical one — with the signs as written, the far-field
Newtonian limit is $\delta\Phi = -\delta Q\,P_2(\cos\theta)/r^3$ with
$\delta Q = +\epsq M^3$ (the $Q_{\rm tot}$ convention of
\eqref{eq:qtot}), and the measured apsidal advance of integrated
orbits matches $-3\pi\epsq(M/p)^2$ for both signs of $\epsq$. That
two-sided empirical pin is the quadrupole-sign test of
Chapter~\ref{ch:devhistory}.

\section{The Bardeen critical curve by elimination}
\label{app:bardeen}

Chapter~\ref{ch:shadow} quotes the parametrisation
\eqref{eq:bardeen}; here is the elimination that produces it. Write
the two shell conditions on the quartic \eqref{eq:bardeenquartic}
with the shorthand $A_c = \xi^2 + \eta$ and
$B_c = \eta + (\xi - a)^2$:
\begin{align}
R/E^2 &= r^4 + (a^2 - A_c)\,r^2 + 2MB_c\,r - a^2\eta = 0,
\label{eq:appbardR}\\
R'/E^2 &= 4r^3 + 2(a^2 - A_c)\,r + 2MB_c = 0 .
\label{eq:appbardRp}
\end{align}
Equation \eqref{eq:appbardRp} is linear in $(a^2 - A_c)$:
\begin{equation}
a^2 - A_c = -2r^2 - \frac{MB_c}{r} .
\label{eq:appbardA}
\end{equation}
Substituting into \eqref{eq:appbardR} collapses it to a relation
linear in $\eta$:
\begin{equation}
r^4 - 2r^4 - MB_c r + 2MB_c r = a^2\eta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\eta = \frac{r\,(MB_c - r^3)}{a^2} .
\label{eq:appbardeta}
\end{equation}
Finally, the definitions of $A_c$ and $B_c$ give
$A_c - B_c = 2a\xi - a^2$, so with \eqref{eq:appbardA},
\begin{equation}
\xi = \frac{A_c - B_c + a^2}{2a}
= \frac{2a^2 + 2r^2 + MB_c/r - B_c}{2a} ,
\label{eq:appbardxi}
\end{equation}
and imposing the remaining consistency $\eta = A_c - \xi^2$ closes
the system into a single quadratic for $B_c(r)$, whose physical root,
back-substituted through
\eqref{eq:appbardeta}--\eqref{eq:appbardxi}, factors into the
compact forms of \eqref{eq:bardeen}. (The factorisation is tedious to
carry by hand but trivial to verify: substituting
\eqref{eq:bardeen} into \eqref{eq:appbardR}--\eqref{eq:appbardRp}
makes both vanish identically as polynomials in $r$ — and the traced
boundary of Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary} performs the same verification
numerically at every point, against the independent integrator.) The
route displays where each qualitative feature comes from: $\eta$ in
\eqref{eq:appbardeta} changes sign where $MB_c = r^3$ — the shell
edges, where the orbit becomes equatorial — and $\xi$ in
\eqref{eq:appbardxi} inherits its $1/a$ prefactor, which is why the
critical curve degenerates to the Synge circle as $a \to 0$ only
through the combination $\xi^2 + \eta$, individual $\xi$ diverging.

\section{The Synge shadow angle at finite radius}
\label{app:synge}

Chapter~\ref{ch:shadow} quotes \eqref{eq:synge}; the derivation is
three steps. A photon at radius $r_{\rm obs}$ in Schwarzschild with
impact parameter $b$ makes a local angle $\alpha$ with the radial
direction given by the tetrad components of its momentum,
\begin{equation}
\sin\alpha
= \frac{k^{\hat\phi}}{\sqrt{(k^{\hat r})^2 + (k^{\hat\phi})^2}}
= \frac{k^{\hat\phi}}{k^{\hat t}}
= \frac{L_z/r}{E\big/\sqrt{1 - 2M/r}}
= \frac{b}{r}\sqrt{1 - \frac{2M}{r}} ,
\label{eq:syngestep}
\end{equation}
using the static-observer tetrad components
($k^{\hat\phi} = L_z/r$, $k^{\hat t} = E/\sqrt{f}$) and, in the second
equality, the null condition
$(k^{\hat t})^2 = (k^{\hat r})^2 + (k^{\hat\phi})^2$ — the relation is
exact, not small-angle. The shadow edge is the
critical impact parameter $b_c = \sqrt{27}\,M$ of the photon sphere,
so
\begin{equation}
\sin\alpha_{\rm sh} = \frac{\sqrt{27}\,M}{r_{\rm obs}}
\sqrt{1 - \frac{2M}{r_{\rm obs}}} ,
\end{equation}
which is \eqref{eq:synge}. Two limits orient the formula: at
$r_{\rm obs} \gg M$ it reduces to the flat-sky $b_c/r_{\rm obs}$
(the correction factor is the $1/\sqrt{1-2M/r}$ of
\eqref{eq:btoalpha}), and at $r_{\rm obs} = 3M$ it gives
$\sin\alpha_{\rm sh} = 1$: an observer \emph{on} the photon sphere
finds the shadow filling exactly half the sky, the memorable
boundary between outside and inside views of the photon shell.
