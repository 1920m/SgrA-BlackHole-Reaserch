\section{What the two channels jointly demonstrate}
\label{sec:consilience}

The central scientific claim of this report is one of
\emph{consilience}: a single Kerr spacetime, with a single mass and
distance, simultaneously reproduces four measurements of entirely
different character. The S2 astrometry and radial velocities return
$M = \FitMass\pm\FitMassErr\times10^6\,\msun$ at
$R_0 = \FitDist\pm\FitDistErr$~pc — dynamical, stellar-scale, built
from twenty-four years of imaging. The GRAVITY redshift excess at the
2018 pericentre emerges from the same fitted orbit at
\GrExcessPeak~km/s with no adjustable amplitude — special plus general
relativity at the $10^{-4}$ level. The Schwarzschild precession
follows at $\PrecSchw'$ per orbit, again amplitude-free. And the same
mass and distance, pushed through the completely independent
photon-orbit machinery of Part~III, predict a photon ring of
$\CalDiamUas\,\muas$ — horizon-scale, radio-interferometric — inside
the EHT band. Nothing was tuned between these comparisons: the chain
from the metric of Chapter~\ref{ch:kerr} to each observable is the
same code, validated by the same gates, and the numbers agree with
four independent observational campaigns spanning six orders of
magnitude in radius. That is far from a proof that the object is a
Kerr black hole — the no-hair interval of Chapter~\ref{ch:nohair} is
wide — but it is precisely the pattern a Kerr black hole predicts, and
each of its alternatives must now reproduce all four numbers at once.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Three idealisations bound the interpretation of the imaging results.
First, the disk is the time-averaged, geometrically thin
Novikov--Thorne solution; the real \sgra{} flow is a hot, geometrically
thick, radiatively inefficient plasma, and our thin-disk images are a
clean GR benchmark rather than a spectral model of \sgra{} itself
(the EHT comparison in Chapter~\ref{ch:calib} concerns the ring
diameter, which is a property of the spacetime, not of the emission
model --- though the ring diameter retains a modest emission-model
dependence through the location of the emitting material). Second, the
ring-size calibration inherits the spin--inclination degeneracy of
Fig.~\ref{fig:sweep}; the quoted agreement holds across the plausible
$(\astar, i)$ range precisely because the observable varies so little.
Third, the orbit fit adopts the \citet{Gillessen2017} compilation and its
error model; systematic reference-frame errors are absorbed by the fitted
frame parameters only to the extent of the \citet{Plewa2015} priors.

Several smaller approximations are worth listing with their sizes,
because each is deliberately \emph{carried}, not overlooked. The
gravitational bending of S2's own light by \sgra{} shifts its apparent
position by of order $20\,\muas$ around pericentre and its radial
velocity by $\sim$5~km\,s$^{-1}$ — both below the imaging-era
uncertainties ($\gtrsim 300\,\muas$, $\gtrsim 15$~km\,s$^{-1}$) and
both neglected here exactly as in \citet{Gravity2018}. S2 itself is
treated as a test particle: at $\sim$$10$--$15\,\msun$ against
$4.3\times10^6\,\msun$, its back-reaction is ten orders below any
observable. Extended mass inside the S-star orbits (a dark cluster,
stellar remnants) is not modelled; the professional fits bound it at
the $\lesssim 10^{-3}\,M$ level, and its principal signature — a
\emph{retrograde} Newtonian precession partially cancelling the
Schwarzschild advance — is the leading systematic on any future
$\epsq$ claim, though not on the mass. And the interstellar
scattering screen toward the Galactic centre, which blurs the EHT
image and is a first-order concern for ring \emph{width} claims,
enters our ring-\emph{diameter} comparison only through the published
EHT deconvolution, whose uncertainty is already inside the quoted
$\pm2.3\,\muas$.

Three further boundaries deserve explicit statement. The bright axial
columns in near-edge-on renders are the lensed polar caustic of
Section~\ref{sec:axialtower} — this model contains no jet physics, and
a viewer must not read Blandford--Znajek outflows into what is pure
geometry; conversely, no conclusion of this report depends on jet
absence. The no-hair reach is bounded by a single star at
$\sim$3000 gravitational radii: the $\sigma(\epsq) \sim 10^{5}$ order
of Chapter~\ref{ch:nohair} is a property of S2's orbit, not of the
method, and improves quadratically with proximity — the cluster
machinery of Chapter~\ref{ch:cluster} is the prepared vehicle for any
confirmed closer star. Finally, the precision split is deliberate: all
quantitative results flow from the fp64 CPU/GPU paths, while the
interactive fp32 path exists for exploration only; the two are never
mixed, and the display-only structure layer of
Chapter~\ref{ch:instrument} sits outside the physics wall entirely.

\section{Outlook}
\label{sec:outlook}

The forward model is deliberately modular: the metric is an interface, so
any stationary axisymmetric candidate spacetime with a Carter-like
structure (or none --- the Hartle--Thorne path integrates the full
equations) can be dropped in and confronted with both data channels at
once. Next-generation instruments motivate exactly this: ngEHT-class
arrays project ring-diameter precision at the per-cent level and access to
the $n=1$ sub-ring \citep{Johnson2020}, while continued GRAVITY astrometry
of S2 --- and any confirmed closer star (S62 remains contested, as noted
in Chapter~\ref{ch:nohair}) --- pushes the stellar channel toward the
spin and quadrupole terms \citep{Will2008,Psaltis2016}.
