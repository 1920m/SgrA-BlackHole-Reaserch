\chapter{The Accretion Disk: Novikov--Thorne and Page--Thorne}
\label{ch:disk}

The disk model supplies every photon's emission point with a temperature
and every pixel with a colour. This chapter derives the model the code
implements (\texttt{flux}, \texttt{temperature}, \texttt{blackbody\_rgb},
\texttt{bolometric} in \texttt{physics/src/disk\_model.cpp}): the
relativistic thin disk of \citet{NovikovThorne1973} with the closed-form
flux of \citet{PageThorne1974}, the exact redshift factor per ray, the
$g^4$ beaming law from Liouville's theorem, and the Planck-to-sRGB
colour pipeline.

\section{Thin-disk conservation laws and the radial structure}
\label{sec:thindiskstructure}

The model disk is geometrically thin, optically thick, and lies in the
equatorial plane between an inner edge at the ISCO and an outer radius
$r_{\rm out}$. Its structure follows from three conservation laws
applied to an annulus $[r, r+\mathrm{d}r]$ in a steady state:
\emph{mass} — the accretion rate $\dot M$ is radius-independent;
\emph{angular momentum} — the inward advection of $L(r)$
(eq.~\ref{eq:lcirc}) is balanced by the viscous torque $W(r)$ between
annuli, less what is radiated; \emph{energy} — the local dissipation is
the difference between the torque's work and the advected binding
energy $E(r)$ (eq.~\ref{eq:ecirc}). Eliminating the torque between the
latter two gives the flux as a single quadrature over the
circular-orbit functions of Chapter~\ref{ch:kerr},
\begin{equation}
F(r) \;\propto\; \frac{\dot M}{r}\,
\Bigl[-\Omega'(r)\Bigr]\,
\bigl[E(r) - \Omega(r) L(r)\bigr]^{-2}
\int_{r_{\rm ISCO}}^{r}
\bigl[E(\tilde r) - \Omega(\tilde r)L(\tilde r)\bigr]\,
L'(\tilde r)\,\mathrm{d}\tilde r ,
\label{eq:ptquadrature}
\end{equation}
each factor with a physical job: $-\Omega'$ is the shear that feeds
the viscous dissipation, $(E - \Omega L)$ is the energy flux carried
per unit angular momentum flux, and the integral accumulates the
angular momentum the gas has had to shed since crossing the inner
edge. The lower limit implements the crucial boundary condition — the
torque \emph{vanishes at the ISCO}, where circular orbits end and
matter plunges ballistically without further contact — so the flux is
pinned to zero at the inner edge, rises to a peak a few gravitational
radii outside it, and decays outward: the single most consequential
qualitative feature of the profile (a disk lit all the way to its
inner edge would look measurably different). Carrying out the
quadrature with the Kerr $E, L, \Omega$ gives the closed form of
\citet[eq.~15n]{PageThorne1974}:
\begin{equation}
F(r) = \frac{3\dot M}{8\pi M^2}\,\frac{f(x)}{x^4},
\qquad x = \sqrt{r/M},
\label{eq:ptflux}
\end{equation}
where the dimensionless $f(x)$ carries all the relativity. The
integrand of \eqref{eq:ptquadrature} becomes rational in
$x = \sqrt{r/M}$ — this is why the substitution is made — and partial
fractions plus term-by-term integration (the full evaluation is
reproduced in Appendix~\ref{app:algebra}) yield the closed form the
code implements:
\begin{equation}
f(x) = \frac{1}{x^{2}\bigl(x^3 - 3x + 2\astar\bigr)}
\Bigl[x - x_0 - \tfrac{3}{2}\astar\ln\frac{x}{x_0}
- \sum_{i=1}^{3}
\frac{3(x_i-\astar)^2}{x_i(x_i-x_j)(x_i-x_k)}
\ln\frac{x-x_i}{x_0-x_i}\Bigr],
\label{eq:pagethorne}
\end{equation}
with $x_0 = \sqrt{r_{\rm ISCO}/M}$ and $x_{1,2,3}$ the roots of the
familiar cubic $x^3 - 3x + 2\astar = 0$ — the same cubic as the photon
orbit \eqref{eq:photoncubic}, solved by the same trigonometric
substitution:
\begin{equation}
x_1 = 2\cos\!\Bigl(\frac{\arccos \astar - \pi}{3}\Bigr),\quad
x_2 = 2\cos\!\Bigl(\frac{\arccos \astar + \pi}{3}\Bigr),\quad
x_3 = -2\cos\!\Bigl(\frac{\arccos \astar}{3}\Bigr).
\label{eq:ptroots}
\end{equation}

Two numerical guards in \texttt{flux} deserve documentation because both
were pinned by unit tests after early mistakes
(Chapter~\ref{ch:devhistory}). First, $x_2 \to 0$ as $\astar \to 0$
while its logarithm coefficient stays finite only through a cancellation
— the Schwarzschild limit must be taken with the $i = 2$ term's
$0\cdot\ln$ product handled explicitly. Second, the far-field behaviour
of the bracket is
\begin{equation}
f(x)\,x^2\bigl(x^3-3x+2\astar\bigr)\big/x^3 \;\longrightarrow\;
x - c + \frac{3}{x} + \mathcal{O}(x^{-2}),
\qquad c \simeq 3.976 \;\;(\astar = 0),
\label{eq:farfield}
\end{equation}
which is \emph{not} the Newtonian $1 - \sqrt{r_{\rm in}/r}$ profile
of folklore: the GR log terms shift the additive constant. The unit
test asserts \eqref{eq:farfield} against the exact expansion rather
than the Newtonian form — an error a visual check could never catch,
since both profiles look identical in a rendered image.

Fig.~\ref{fig:diskprofiles} shows $F(r)$ and the effective temperature
$T_{\rm eff} \propto F^{1/4}$ across the spin range: as the ISCO
plunges inward with spin (Chapter~\ref{ch:kerr}), the emitting annulus
extends deeper, the peak flux grows, and the peak moves to smaller
radii — the radial anatomy behind the spin-morphology discussion of
Section~\ref{sec:spinmorphology}.

\section{The redshift factor}
\label{sec:gfactor}

A backward-traced ray that crosses the equatorial annulus at radius $r$
is assigned the frequency ratio between the camera and the co-orbiting
emitter. The emitter moves on a circular orbit with angular velocity
$\Omega(r)$ (eq.~\ref{eq:omegacirc}) and four-velocity
$u^\mu = u^t(1, 0, 0, \Omega)$, normalised by
\begin{equation}
u^t = \bigl[-(g_{tt} + 2\Omega g_{t\phi}
+ \Omega^2 g_{\phi\phi})\bigr]^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
The emitted frequency is $\nu_{\rm emit} = -k_\mu u^\mu
= u^t (E - \Omega L_z)$ (using $k_t = -E$, $k_\phi = L_z$), while the
camera normalisation fixes $\nu_{\rm obs} = 1$; hence
\begin{equation}
g \equiv \frac{\nu_{\rm obs}}{\nu_{\rm emit}}
      = \frac{1}{u^t\,(E - \Omega L_z)}
      = \frac{\sqrt{-(g_{tt} + 2\Omega g_{t\phi} + \Omega^2
        g_{\phi\phi})}}{1 - \Omega\,b_\gamma},
\qquad b_\gamma = \frac{L_z}{E}.
\label{eq:gfactor}
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:gfactor} packages all three redshifts of the primer
(Chapter~\ref{ch:f4}) in one exact expression: the numerator carries
gravitational redshift and transverse Doppler (it is $1/u^t$, the
emitter's total time dilation), while the denominator
$1 - \Omega b_\gamma$ is the line-of-sight Doppler term — photons
emitted forward along the orbital motion ($\Omega b_\gamma > 0$) are
blueshifted, backward redshifted. For the Kerr circular orbits feeding
the disk, the numerator is available in closed form: the normalisation
worked through \citet{BardeenPressTeukolsky1972} gives
\begin{equation}
u^t =
\frac{1 \pm a\sqrt{M/r^3}}{\sqrt{1 - 3M/r \pm 2a\sqrt{M/r^3}}}
\qquad\text{(upper sign prograde)},
\label{eq:utcirc}
\end{equation}
which at $a = 0$ reduces to the memorable
$u^t = (1 - 3M/r)^{-1/2}$: a clock on a circular orbit at the ISCO of
a Schwarzschild hole runs slow by $\sqrt{1 - 1/2} = 0.707$ — half its
time-dilation from depth, half from speed, the two contributions the
primer's Chapter~\ref{ch:f4} kept separate now fused into one exact
factor. The divergence of \eqref{eq:utcirc} as $r$ approaches the
photon orbit (where the denominator vanishes) is physical: circular
orbits become null there, and the formal $g \to 0$ says any emitter
forced onto them would be infinitely dimmed. The disk never reaches
that regime — it ends at the ISCO — but rays that skim the photon
shell \emph{between} disk crossings do, which is one reason
higher-order sub-ring images are faint beyond their geometric
demagnification. In the reference render at
$\astar = 0.94$, $i = 80\degr$ the factor spans $g = \GMin$ to
$\GMax$ across the image.

\section{The $g^4$ beaming law from Liouville's theorem}
\label{sec:gfour}

Why does observed brightness scale as the \emph{fourth} power of $g$?
The clean argument is kinetic: photons in a beam occupy a phase-space
volume, and Liouville's theorem makes the phase-space density invariant
along rays — in radiative-transfer language, $I_\nu/\nu^3$ is the same
in every frame that watches the beam pass (each factor of $\nu$: one
from the energy per photon, two from the solid-angle compression of the
momentum cell). Integrating over frequency,
\begin{equation}
I_{\rm obs} = \int I_{\nu,\rm obs}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\rm obs}
= \int g^3 I_{\nu/g,\rm emit}\; g\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{\rm emit}
= g^4\,I_{\rm emit}.
\label{eq:gfour}
\end{equation}
For a blackbody there is an equivalent and computationally convenient
statement: a Planck spectrum at temperature $T$, every photon of which
is scaled by $g$, is \emph{exactly} a Planck spectrum at temperature
$gT$ — blackbodies have no spectral features to distort. The code
exploits this: the observed bolometric intensity is
$(g\,T_{\rm eff}/T_{\rm ref})^4$ (\texttt{bolometric}), and the observed
\emph{colour} is the blackbody colour at $g\,T_{\rm eff}$ — no separate
beaming factor is ever applied to the RGB channel, because in-band
spectral radiance does not scale as $g^4$; only the bolometric total
does. Keeping these two channels distinct (colour from $g T$;
quantitative flux from $g^4$) is what lets the same render serve both as
a faithful image and as the measurement input for the beaming-asymmetry
and sub-ring statistics.

The measured consequence (Gate 3): the approaching limb outshines the
receding limb by a bolometric factor of \BeamRatio{} in the reference
geometry — the asymmetry every real black-hole image shows, and a hard
acceptance criterion of the build (a symmetric disk means a broken
$g^4$).

\section{Planck spectrum to display colour}
\label{sec:cie}

The rendered colours are physical. For a temperature $T$ the spectral
radiance is Planck's law; folding it against the CIE 1931 colour-matching
functions (the analytic multi-lobe Gaussian fits of
\citealt{WymanSloanShirley2013}) gives tristimulus values $(X, Y, Z)$,
which a linear transform takes to linear sRGB
(\texttt{planck\_to\_linear\_srgb}, LUT-accelerated in
\texttt{blackbody\_rgb}). Fig.~\ref{fig:cielocus} shows the resulting
Planckian locus and the display colours across temperature — the
ember-orange to blue-white thermometer of the primer, now quantitative.
The disk's inner annuli at $g T_{\rm eff} \sim 10^4$~K render
blue-white; the beamed approaching limb is pushed further blueward
exactly as \eqref{eq:gfactor} dictates.

Two implementation details bound the pipeline's domain, both visible
in \texttt{disk\_model.cpp}. The blackbody LUT is log-spaced over
$500$--$60\,000$~K ($512$ entries of the exact
\texttt{planck\_to\_linear\_srgb} evaluation), and pixels whose
$g\,T_{\rm eff}$ falls below $505$~K return black: below that
temperature the visible-band radiance of a blackbody is negligible at
any exposure the renders use, so the floor discards nothing
observable. At the top, $60\,000$~K comfortably exceeds the hottest
$g\,T_{\rm eff}$ any production configuration produces (beamed inner
annuli at high spin); chromaticity also saturates along the Planckian
locus above $\sim$$2\times10^4$~K — hotter only gets marginally bluer
— so LUT resolution there is uncritical. Both bounds are asserted,
not assumed: the colour unit test sweeps the LUT against direct
evaluation, and the ``blackbody colours behave physically'' test pins
monotonicity of the red-to-blue progression
(Chapter~\ref{ch:devhistory}).

\section{The temperature scale, and what the model does not claim}
\label{sec:tempscale}

One honest caveat belongs in the derivation chapter rather than the
small print. The Page--Thorne flux \eqref{eq:ptflux} carries the
accretion rate $\dot M$ as an overall factor, and this project makes
no claim about the true $\dot M$ of \sgra{}. The implementation
(\texttt{NovikovThorneDisk}) instead exposes a display normalisation:
the constructor scans the flux profile, finds its maximum, and sets
the temperature scale so that the \emph{peak} effective temperature
equals a chosen $T_{\rm peak}$; $T_{\rm eff}(r) \propto F(r)^{1/4}$
then fixes every other annulus relative to it. Every quantitative
claim this report makes from disk renders is therefore a \emph{shape}
or \emph{ratio} statement — the beaming contrast \BeamRatio{}, the
$g$-range $[\GMin, \GMax]$, sub-ring flux ratios, the radial profile
against \eqref{eq:farfield} — in which the normalisation cancels;
none is an absolute-luminosity statement, which would require the real
accretion rate. This division of labour is deliberate and mirrors the
physical situation: the \emph{geometry} of the image (shadow, critical
curve, lensed arches) is pure spacetime, the \emph{kinematic} pattern
(beaming, redshift gradients) is pure orbital mechanics, and only the
absolute brightness depends on the messy accretion physics that the
thin-disk idealisation cannot capture for \sgra{} anyway — the real
flow there is a hot, radiatively inefficient structure accreting far
below the Eddington rate \citep{Broderick2014, EHT2022}, not a cold
thin disk. Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion} weighs which conclusions
survive that difference.

\section{Spin morphology of the disk image}
\label{sec:spinmorphology}

A striking qualitative observation from interactive exploration with
the live renderer (Chapter~\ref{ch:instrument}): at $\astar = 0$ the
disk reads as a system of smooth, continuous, near-concentric bright
lanes girdling the whole silhouette, while at high spin
($\astar \approx 0.9$) the coherent bright structure collapses to a
compact, intense patch on the approaching limb. Three exact results of
this report combine to explain it:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The emission zone deepens.} The ISCO falls from $6M$ to
$\sim 2.3M$ between $\astar = 0$ and $0.9$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:radiispin}), and the Page--Thorne peak follows it
inward (Fig.~\ref{fig:diskprofiles}) — the brightest material moves to
radii where orbital speeds approach half the speed of light.
\item \emph{Beaming sharpens as $g^4$.} With $v/c$ rising steeply at
the new inner radii, the approaching/receding contrast
\eqref{eq:gfour} grows from a moderate factor to orders of magnitude;
the receding-side lanes fall below any practical display window while
the approaching side saturates.
\item \emph{Frame dragging compresses the pattern.} The display-layer
streamlines advect at the true $\Omega(r)$ of \eqref{eq:omegacirc};
its steepening differential shear at small $r$ winds features tightly
on the prograde side.
\end{enumerate}

At $\astar = 0$ none of these amplifiers operates strongly — orbital
speeds at $r \gtrsim 6M$ stay below $0.4c$, the beaming contrast is
mild, and the lanes remain visible around the full circumference. The
transition between the two regimes is thus a direct visualisation of
the ISCO physics of Chapter~\ref{ch:kerr}.
