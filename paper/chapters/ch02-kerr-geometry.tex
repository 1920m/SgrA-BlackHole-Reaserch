\section{The Kerr solution in Boyer--Lindquist coordinates}
\label{sec:kerrmetric}

In Boyer--Lindquist (BL) coordinates with $G=c=1$ and spin parameter
$a = J/M$, the Kerr line element is
\begin{align}
\mathrm{d}s^2 ={}& -\left(1-\frac{2Mr}{\Sigma}\right)\mathrm{d}t^2
 - \frac{4Mar\sin^2\theta}{\Sigma}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\phi
 + \frac{\Sigma}{\Delta}\,\mathrm{d}r^2 \nonumber\\
&{}+ \Sigma\,\mathrm{d}\theta^2
 + \frac{A\sin^2\theta}{\Sigma}\,\mathrm{d}\phi^2,
\label{eq:kerr}
\end{align}
with $\Sigma = r^2+a^2\cos^2\theta$, $\Delta = r^2-2Mr+a^2$ and
$A = (r^2+a^2)^2 - a^2\Delta\sin^2\theta$. This is the form implemented by
\texttt{kerr\_components} in \texttt{physics/src/metric.cpp}, and the
determinant identity $\det g = -\Sigma^2\sin^2\theta$ is asserted by a
dedicated unit test (Chapter~\ref{ch:devhistory}).

\stub{Kerr--Schild form and the BL$\leftrightarrow$KS transformation;
inverse metric; Killing vectors $\partial_t, \partial_\phi$; the
Killing--Yano and Killing tensors as the origin of the Carter constant;
frame dragging and the ZAMO angular velocity
$\omega = -g_{t\phi}/g_{\phi\phi}$.}

\section{Horizons, ergosphere, and characteristic radii}
\label{sec:radii}

The outer horizon lies at
$r_+ = M+\sqrt{M^2-a^2}$, the ergosphere at
$r_E(\theta) = M+\sqrt{M^2-a^2\cos^2\theta}$, and the equatorial photon
orbits at
$r_{\rm ph}^{\pm} = 2M\{1+\cos[\tfrac{2}{3}\cos^{-1}(\mp a/M)]\}$
(prograde/retrograde). The innermost stable circular orbit follows the
closed form of \citet{BardeenPressTeukolsky1972},
\begin{align}
Z_1 &= 1 + (1-\astar^2)^{1/3}\left[(1+\astar)^{1/3} +
      (1-\astar)^{1/3}\right],\nonumber\\
Z_2 &= \sqrt{3\astar^2 + Z_1^2},\nonumber\\
r_{\rm ISCO} &= M\left[3 + Z_2 \mp
      \sqrt{(3-Z_1)(3+Z_1+2Z_2)}\right],
\label{eq:isco}
\end{align}
with the upper sign for prograde orbits. At $\astar \equiv a/M = 0$ these
reduce to $2M$, $2M$, $3M$ and $6M$ exactly, which the test suite asserts
to machine precision before any imaging is attempted
(Chapter~\ref{ch:devhistory}); for $\astar > 0$ it asserts the
prograde/retrograde split (e.g.\ distinct photon-orbit radii at
$\astar = 0.5$ and $0.94$) and the monotonic approach
$r_{\rm ISCO} \to r_+$ as $\astar \to 1$. These radii are implemented by
\texttt{r\_horizon}, \texttt{r\_ergosphere}, \texttt{r\_photon} and
\texttt{r\_isco} in \texttt{metric.cpp}.

\stub{Full algebra: equatorial photon orbits from the effective-potential
cubic and its trigonometric solution; the BPT ISCO derivation from
$\mathrm{d}^2V/\mathrm{d}r^2 = 0$ through $Z_1, Z_2$; the marginally bound
radius $r_{mb}$. New figure: characteristic radii vs $\astar$ (needs
\texttt{physics\_tables}).}
