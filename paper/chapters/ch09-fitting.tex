\chapter{Orbit Fitting, Statistics, and the S2 Results}
\label{ch:fitting}

\section{Data}
\label{sec:data}

The astrometric and spectroscopic record of S2 is taken from the
monitoring compilation of \citet{Gillessen2017} (VizieR
J/ApJ/837/30): \FitNAst{} imaging positions (VLT NACO and Keck NIRC,
1992--2016, typical uncertainties 0.3--3~mas) and \FitNRv{} radial
velocities (SINFONI and earlier spectrographs, 2000--2016, typical
uncertainties 15--100~km\,s$^{-1}$). Positions are offsets from the radio
position of \sgra{}; the residual uncertainty of that frame tie is
modelled by the fitted offset/drift parameters with the
\citet{Plewa2015} priors of Section~\ref{sec:fitter}. We apply no error
rescaling: the reduced $\chi^2$ of \FitChiRed{} below is obtained with
the published uncertainties as-is.

\section{The objective and its parameterisation}
\label{sec:fitter}

The objective is
\begin{align}
\chi^2 ={}& \sum_i \left[
  \frac{(\alpha_i^{\rm obs}-\alpha_i^{\rm mod})^2}{\sigma_{\alpha,i}^2}
 +\frac{(\delta_i^{\rm obs}-\delta_i^{\rm mod})^2}{\sigma_{\delta,i}^2}
 \right]
 \nonumber\\
 &{}+ \sum_j
  \frac{(v_{r,j}^{\rm obs}-v_{r,j}^{\rm mod})^2}{\sigma_{v,j}^2}
 + \chi^2_{\rm prior},
\label{eq:chi2}
\end{align}
over the \FitNAst{} astrometric epochs and \FitNRv{} radial velocities,
with the forward model of Chapter~\ref{ch:sstar} supplying the model
positions and velocities.

Thirteen parameters are fitted: $(M, R_0)$, six orbital elements, the four
reference-frame offsets and drifts $(x_0, y_0, v_x, v_y)$ of the mass with
respect to the radio frame, and $v_{z}$; the no-hair mode adds $\epsq$.
Weak priors tie the frame to \sgra{} following \citet{Plewa2015}
($\pm0.2$~mas at epoch 2009.0, $\pm0.1$~mas\,yr$^{-1}$,
$\pm5$~km\,s$^{-1}$). The $\chi^2$ is minimised with the Ceres solver
(automatic differentiation; \texttt{OrbitFitter::fit}); parameter
covariances are obtained as
$C = (J^{\mathsf T} J)^{-1}$ from an explicit central-difference Jacobian
$J$ of the weighted residual vector (\texttt{OrbitFitter::covariance}),
because the solver's truncated-SVD
covariance annihilates the strongly correlated $M$--$R_0$ direction and
reports a spuriously zero mass uncertainty
(Chapter~\ref{ch:devhistory}).

\subsection{Fisher information and the Cram\'er--Rao floor}
\label{sec:fisher}

For a model with Gaussian errors, write the $\sigma$-normalised residual
vector $r(\theta)$ and its Jacobian $J_{ik} = \partial r_i/\partial
\theta_k$. Near the optimum, $\chi^2(\theta) \approx \chi^2_{\min} +
\delta\theta^{\mathsf T}(J^{\mathsf T}J)\,\delta\theta$, so
$F = J^{\mathsf T}J$ is the Fisher information matrix, and the
Cram\'er--Rao bound states that \emph{no} unbiased estimator can beat
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Cov}(\theta) \succeq F^{-1} = (J^{\mathsf T} J)^{-1}.
\label{eq:cramerrao}
\end{equation}
For a least-squares estimator at a well-conditioned optimum the bound is
attained to linear order, which is why $C = (J^{\mathsf T}J)^{-1}$ is
the standard parameter covariance; every ``$\pm$'' in
Table~\ref{tab:fit} is the square root of a diagonal element of exactly
this matrix, built from an explicit central-difference $J$ with
physically scaled steps and inverted by Gauss--Jordan elimination with
partial pivoting (\texttt{OrbitFitter::covariance}).

\subsection{Why the solver's SVD covariance fails here}
\label{sec:svdfail}

The subtlety is the spectrum of $F$. Astrometry alone constrains the
\emph{angular} orbit — approximately the combination
$M/R_0^3$ through Kepler's law with the angular semi-major axis and the
period — while the radial velocities pin the \emph{physical} velocity
scale $\propto \sqrt{M/R_0}$. The two constraints intersect at a sharp
angle in the $(M, R_0)$ plane: the fit is superbly conditioned along
one combination and weakly conditioned along the near-degenerate one,
so $F$ carries one singular value several orders below the rest —
small, but genuinely finite and \emph{information-bearing}. A
covariance routine that regularises by \emph{truncating} small singular
values treats that direction as exact rank deficiency: the truncated
pseudo-inverse assigns it \emph{zero} variance, and the reported mass
uncertainty collapses to machine noise. That is precisely the failure
observed with the library covariance during Phase 4 (a fitted mass
``known'' to nine digits was the giveaway; Chapter~\ref{ch:devhistory})
and the reason the project computes \eqref{eq:cramerrao} explicitly:
the small singular value is \emph{inverted}, not discarded, and the
$M$--$R_0$ error ellipse comes out with its true, elongated shape —
subsequently confirmed end-to-end by the MCMC posterior of
Section~\ref{sec:mcmc}.

\label{sec:s2fit}

(Fig.~\ref{fig:orbit}, Fig.~\ref{fig:rv}) has

\section{Gravitational redshift at pericentre}
\label{sec:redshift}

The magnitude of the relativistic signal at pericentre follows from the
fitted elements alone. Step by step:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The pericentre distance is
$r_{\rm peri} = a(1-e) = 1032\,\mathrm{au}\times(1-\FitEcc)
 \approx 121$~au, i.e.\ $\approx 1470$ Schwarzschild radii
($R_S = 2GM/c^2 \approx 0.085$~au).
\item The vis-viva speed there is
$v_{\rm peri} = \sqrt{GM(2/r_{\rm peri}-1/a)}
 \approx 7650\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, so $\beta \approx 0.0255$.
\item The transverse Doppler shift contributes
$z_{\rm tD} \simeq \beta^2/2 \approx 3.3\times10^{-4}$, i.e.\
$c\,z_{\rm tD} \approx 98\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$.
\item The gravitational redshift contributes
$z_{\rm grav} \simeq GM/(r_{\rm peri}c^2) = \beta^2_{\rm circ}
 \approx 3.5\times10^{-4}$, i.e.\ $\approx 105\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$.
\end{enumerate}
The two corrections are nearly equal at pericentre --- not a coincidence:
vis-viva gives $v_{\rm peri}^2 = (GM/a)(1+e)/(1-e)$ while
$GM/r_{\rm peri} = (GM/a)/(1-e)$, so
$z_{\rm grav}/z_{\rm tD} = 2/(1+e) = 1.06$ at the S2 eccentricity --- and
their sum in the full geodesic model peaks at
$\GrExcessPeak\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ at epoch \GrExcessEpoch{}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:redshift}), in agreement with the
$\approx 200\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ excess measured around the 2018.38
pericentre passage by \citet{Gravity2018}, who quantified it as
$f = 0.90\pm0.17$ on a scale where $f=0$ is Newtonian and $f=1$ is GR.
Our forward model contains no adjustable redshift amplitude: the excess is
forced by the fitted mass and geometry. (Like \citet{Gravity2018} we
neglect the $\sim$5~km\,s$^{-1}$ light-bending correction.)

\section{Relativistic precession}
\label{sec:precession}

The fitted orbit advances its pericentre by $\PrecSchw$~arcmin per orbital
period from the Schwarzschild term. The textbook estimate agrees step by
step: the semilatus rectum is
$p = a(1-e^2) = 1032\,\mathrm{au} \times (1-\FitEcc^2) \approx 228$~au,
so with $R_g \approx 0.0424$~au the ratio is
$R_g/p = 1.86\times10^{-4}$ and
\begin{equation}
\delta\omega = \frac{6\pi GM}{c^2 a (1-e^2)} = 6\pi\,\frac{R_g}{p}
 \approx 3.5\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{rad},
\end{equation}
i.e.\ $\approx 12.0$~arcmin per 16-yr orbit, matching the full geodesic
integration ($\PrecSchw$~arcmin) and the GRAVITY detection of the
Schwarzschild precession, $f_{\rm SP} = 1.10\pm0.19$ \citep{Gravity2020}.
Frame dragging at $\astar = 0.94$ contributes only $\PrecLT$~arcmin per
orbit (retrograde for the S2 geometry), two orders of magnitude below
current sensitivity, consistent with the hierarchy laid out by
\citet{Will2008}.

Fig.~\ref{fig:precdecomp} places the three analytic precession terms of
\texttt{GRPrecession} side by side for the fitted S2 elements as a
function of hypothetical spin: the Schwarzschild advance dominates by
two orders of magnitude over Lense--Thirring frame dragging, itself two
orders above the Kerr quadrupole term — the hierarchy of
\citet{Will2008} that dictates what each observing generation can
reach, and the reason the no-hair constraint of
Chapter~\ref{ch:nohair} is bounded by the quadrupole rung of this
ladder.

\section{MCMC posteriors}
\label{sec:mcmc}

The covariance of Section~\ref{sec:fisher} is a \emph{local, Gaussian}
statement. To validate it globally — and to expose any non-Gaussian
tails or multi-modality the linearisation would hide — the same forward
model is sampled with Markov chain Monte Carlo
(\texttt{scripts/run\_mcmc.py}).

\subsection{Sampler}

We use the affine-invariant ensemble sampler of
\citet{GoodmanWeare2010} as implemented in \texttt{emcee}
\citep{ForemanMackey2013}: an ensemble of walkers in which each is
updated by a ``stretch move'' along the line toward a randomly chosen
partner, with a step-size distribution that makes the algorithm
invariant under all affine reparameterisations. Affine invariance is
exactly the property this problem wants — the posterior's dominant
feature is the elongated, tilted $M$--$R_0$ ellipse of
Section~\ref{sec:svdfail}, which a naive Metropolis sampler with
axis-aligned proposals would traverse painfully slowly, but which a
stretch move handles as easily as a sphere.

The log-probability is $-\tfrac12\chi^2$ evaluated by the \emph{same
production code path} as the least-squares fit: a persistent
\texttt{fit\_s2\_tool --chi2} server process receives parameter vectors
on standard input and returns $\chi^2$ per line, so the posterior and
the Ceres optimum are directly comparable with no re-implementation
risk. Eight server processes evaluate a whole ensemble batch in
parallel (3.7~ms per evaluation; a full production run takes seconds).
Priors are flat within generous physical bounds; the
\citet{Plewa2015} reference-frame priors are already inside $\chi^2$.

\subsection{Convergence and results}

The production chain runs \McmcNWalkers{} walkers for \McmcNSteps{}
steps from a small ball around the least-squares optimum. The mean
acceptance fraction is $0.72$ and the longest integrated
autocorrelation time across parameters is
$\tau_{\max} \approx \McmcTauMax{}$ steps — the chain is
$\sim$$10^2\,\tau$ long, comfortably beyond the
$50\,\tau$ convergence rule of thumb \citep{ForemanMackey2013}.
Discarding $4\tau_{\max}$ as burn-in and thinning by $\tau_{\max}/2$
leaves $6\,336$ effectively independent posterior samples.

The marginal posteriors give
$M = \McmcMassMed^{+\McmcMassHi}_{-\McmcMassLo}\times10^{6}\,\msun$ and
$R_0 = \McmcDistMed^{+\McmcDistHi}_{-\McmcDistLo}$~pc (16th/50th/84th
percentiles) — in agreement with the least-squares values and the
explicit-Jacobian uncertainties of Table~\ref{tab:fit}, which is the
cross-validation this section exists to provide: the local Gaussian
covariance and the global sampled posterior tell the same story, so
neither the truncated-SVD pathology nor any hidden non-Gaussianity
survives in the quoted errors. The full corner plot
(Fig.~\ref{fig:corner}) shows every pairwise marginal: the $M$--$R_0$
and $M$--$a_{\rm mas}$ panels carry the expected strong, near-linear
correlations; all distributions are single-moded and near-Gaussian.
